## What to Skip

- Avoid no-name Amazon blenders with generic stock photos and zero brand history. They're often the same blender under five different labels, and replacement parts are impossible to find.
- Skip anything advertising "1000W" for under $30. Real wattage costs money, and inflated numbers on cheap blenders rarely match actual blending performance.
- Don't buy a blender without checking if replacement cups or blades are sold separately. Cheap blenders die fastest at the blade seal, and if you can't replace it, you're buying a new one.
