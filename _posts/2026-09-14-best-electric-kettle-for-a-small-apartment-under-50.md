## What to Skip

- Avoid kettles with no auto shut-off or boil-dry protection, no matter how cheap. It's a basic safety feature, not a luxury add-on.
- Skip anything advertised as "1.7L" or larger if your kitchen is genuinely tight. The bigger tank means a bigger base, and that footprint adds up fast on a small counter.
- Be wary of ultra-cheap plastic-bodied kettles under $20. They tend to develop a plastic smell in hot water within a few months.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What size electric kettle is best for a small apartment?**
Somewhere between 0.5L and 1L covers most single or double-occupant needs without taking up unnecessary counter or cabinet space. If you're mostly making single mugs of tea or coffee, 0.5-0.8L is plenty.

**Are cheap electric kettles under $50 safe to use?**
Yes, as long as they include auto shut-off and boil-dry protection, both of which are standard on every kettle featured above. The main risk with ultra-budget kettles is skipping these features entirely, so always check before buying.

**Do I need an electric kettle with temperature control?**
Only if you're particular about tea steeping temperatures or pour-over coffee. For basic boiling, a simple on/off kettle works fine and tends to cost less.
