## How We Picked These

I prioritized blade sharpness and consistency first, since a dull mandoline is more dangerous than a sharp one: it makes you push harder, and that's how fingers end up where blades are. After that, I looked at finger guard design, ease of cleaning, and whether the thing stores flat without eating your whole cabinet. Price under $50 was non-negotiable, and I leaned toward brands with a track record of replacement parts in case a blade dulls or a guard cracks.

## What to Skip

- Avoid mandolines with finger guards that only cover the palm. If your fingertips are exposed while gripping produce, it's a matter of when, not if, you'll nick yourself.
- Skip anything with a non-removable blade. You can't sharpen most budget mandoline blades, and if you can't swap them out, the whole tool becomes trash once it dulls.
- Pass on models with no non-slip base. A mandoline that shifts on the counter while you're pushing produce through it is a recipe for an ER visit.
